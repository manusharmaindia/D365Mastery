<p>Bank Reconciliation is a periodic process which helps to Match the Bank Account balance as per the ERP (books of accounts) with the Balance as per the Bank.</p><p>Why the difference arises?</p><p>First Category of Differences - There are possibility that some of the transactions that are entered in the bank are not entered in the ERP for example</p><p>1. A customer deposited money in the bank but did not inform the ERP team of our (let's say USMF) company.</p><p>2. Dividend from subsidiary credited to the bank but the ERP team missed to enter in the Books of accounts</p><p>3. Some Charges / Interest charged by the Bank but not entered in the ERP.</p><p>Second Category of Differences - There are possibility that some of the transactions are entered by the ERP team but those are not entered in the Bank for example</p><p>1. Customer check Deposited to Bank but was not cleared.</p><p>2. We issued check to vendor on 31st March but vendor submitted and get it cleared on 1st April.</p><p>Bank reconciliation helps us to manage these differences.</p><p><br /></p><p>There are some questions which used to come into my mind when I used to prepare the Bank Reconciliation.</p><p>Qns 1. When preparing the bank statement if we found some transaction which get cleared in next period for example vendor was issued check (as discussed above) then should we reverse the transaction and report it again in the next period to make the balance of the bank matching with the books of accounts (ERP Balance).</p><p>Ans 1. </p><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">Reversing an outstanding check and re-posting it in the next month is not the correct accounting practice. Doing so would misrepresent your financial position, distort vendor aging, and create audit trails that are difficult to defend.</p><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">Here is the breakdown of why this shouldn't be reversed and how the accounting logic works in Dynamics 365 Finance.</p><h3Google Sans", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;"><bGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">1. Why You Should Never "Reverse and Re-post"</b></h3><ulGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important; padding-inline-start: 32px;"><li><p><b>Accuracy of Dates:</b> Accounting rules require you to record a payment on the day the check is issued (the day you no longer have control over those funds). If you wrote the check on March 30, it must be recorded in March.</p></li><li><p><b>Vendor Aging:</b> If you reverse the check on March 31, the vendor invoice will suddenly appear "unpaid" on your March financial reports, even though you have already sent the payment.</p></li><li><p><b>Audit Trail:</b> Reversing a valid payment simply to make a reconciliation screen "match" creates "noise" in the ledger. Auditors look for "reversals" as potential red flags for fraud or error correction.</p></li></ul><h3Google Sans", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;"><bGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">2. The Accounting Flow</b></h3><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">When you post a check in D365, the following happens immediately in the General Ledger:</p><tableGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-bottom: 32px; margin-top: 0px !important;"><thead><tr><td><strong>Account</strong></td><td><strong>Debit</strong></td><td><strong>Credit</strong></td></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><b>Vendor Summary Account</b> (Accounts Payable)</td><td>$1,000</td><td></td></tr><tr><td><b>Bank Main Account</b> (Asset)</td><td></td><td>$1,000</td></tr></tbody></table><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">At this moment, your <b>Book Balance</b> (the balance in D365) has decreased. However, your <b>Bank Balance</b> (the money actually in the bank) will not decrease until the vendor deposits that check and the bank processes it.</p><h3Google Sans", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;"><bGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">3. How the Reconciliation Handles the Difference</b></h3><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">The purpose of a Bank Reconciliation is not to make the Book Balance match the Statement Balance <i>on the ledger</i>; it is to <b>explain the difference</b> between the two.</p><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">In the <b>Manual (Simple) Bank Reconciliation</b> form in D365:</p><ol start="1"Google Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important; padding-inline-start: 32px;"><li><p><b>Statement Ending Balance:</b> You enter the balance from your paper statement (e.g., $10,000).</p></li><li><p><b>Transactions List:</b> You see all posted D365 transactions. You tick the ones that appear on the statement.</p></li><li><p><b>Outstanding Items:</b> You leave the $1,000 check <b>un-ticked</b> (Uncleared).</p></li><li><p><b>The Logic:</b> D365 performs this internal calculation:</p><ul><li><p><i>Statement Ending Balance ($10,000)</i></p></li><li><p><i>Minus Outstanding Checks ($1,000)</i></p></li><li><p><i>Equals <b>Calculated Book Balance ($9,000)</b></i></p></li></ul></li></ol><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">If your actual <b>D365 Bank Ledger Balance</b> is $9,000, the "Unreconciled" field at the bottom of the screen will show <b>0.00</b>. This means you are "balanced," even though the two primary numbers ($10k vs $9k) are different.</p><h3Google Sans", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;"><bGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">4. What Happens Next Month?</b></h3><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">When you start the reconciliation for the next month:</p><ulGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important; padding-inline-start: 32px;"><li><p>The check you didn't tick last month <b>automatically carries forward</b>. It will stay in your "Uncleared" list month after month.</p></li><li><p>The moment that check appears on a bank statement (perhaps in April), you simply tick "Cleared" during the April reconciliation.</p></li><li><p>The check then moves from "Outstanding" to "Reconciled," and it will no longer appear in future reconciliation windows.</p></li></ul><h3Google Sans", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;"><br /></h3><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;">Do not be tempted to reverse transactions to make the balances match. The reconciliation's job is to document these 'timing differences.' In D365, as long as your <b>Unreconciled amount is zero</b>, your books are correct. The uncleared check remains on the 'Right Side' of the reconciliation worksheet as an outstanding item until the bank statement finally catches up.</p><pGoogle Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important;"><br /></p><h3Google Sans Text", sans-serif !important; line-height: 1.15 !important; margin-top: 0px !important; text-align: left;"><b>But what balance will go to financial statement then?</b></h3><div _ngcontent-ng-c1098760420=""><message-content _ngcontent-ng-c1098760420="" _nghost-ng-c1840272799="" id="message-content-id-r_854206b08f774d5d"><div _ngcontent-ng-c1840272799="" dir="ltr" id="model-response-message-contentr_854206b08f774d5d" inline-copy-host=""><p>The balance that appears on your financial statements (Balance Sheet and Trial Balance) is always the <b>General Ledger (GL) Book Balance</b>, not the bank statement balance.</p><p>In accounting, your financial statements must reflect the transactions the company has authorized and executed. Here is how that works in practice and why it is the correct approach:</p><h3><b>1. The GL Balance is the "Source of Truth"</b></h3><p>When you generate a Balance Sheet in Dynamics 365 Finance, the system looks at the <b>Main Account</b> assigned to that bank.</p><ul><li><p>Because you posted the check at the end of the month, the ledger has already been credited (reduced).</p></li><li><p>Therefore, your financial statements show the reduced amount.</p></li></ul><p><b>Example:</b></p><ul><li><p><b>Bank Statement Balance:</b> $10,000</p></li><li><p><b>Outstanding Check:</b> $1,000 (Sent to a vendor on the 30th)</p></li><li><p><b>Balance Sheet (GL) Amount:</b> <b>$9,000</b></p></li></ul><p>The $9,000 is what goes to your financial statements. This is correct because, from an accounting perspective, you no longer "own" that $1,000; it has been committed to a vendor.</p><h3><b>2. Why the Bank Statement Balance is NOT used</b></h3><p>If you reported the bank statement balance of $10,000 on your Balance Sheet, you would be <b>overstating your assets</b>. You would be claiming you have $10,000 available to spend, when in reality, $1,000 of that is already promised to a vendor who holds a legal document (the check) to claim it.</p><h3><b>3. The Role of the Bank Reconciliation Report</b></h3><p>If an auditor or a manager asks, "Why does the Balance Sheet say $9,000 when the bank says we have $10,000?", you provide the <b>Bank Reconciliation Report</b>.</p><p>The reconciliation report acts as a "bridge" or a supporting schedule that proves the $9,000 on the financial statement is accurate. It says:</p><ul><li><p>"We show $9,000 because we know about a $1,000 check that hasn't reached the bank yet."</p></li></ul><h3><b>4. Summary of the Flow</b></h3><ol start="1"><li><p><b>General Ledger:</b> Tracks every transaction the moment it happens. This balance is used for <b>Financial Reporting</b>.</p></li><li><p><b>Bank Statement:</b> Tracks only what the bank has processed.</p></li><li><p><b>Bank Reconciliation:</b> A control document used to verify that the difference between the two is legitimate (e.g., outstanding checks, deposits in transit, or bank errors).</p></li></ol><p><b>Accounting Rule:</b> You report what you <i>know</i> (the Ledger), and you reconcile it to what the <i>external party</i> knows (the Bank).</p></div></message-content><!----></div><p>--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------</p><p>There are two types of reconciliation in D365: -</p><p>1. Simple Bank Reconciliation (Manual Reconciliation) - User manually reconcile the transactions</p><p>2. Advance Bank Reconciliation (Automatic Reconciliation) - System performs the initial reconciliation and at the end when there are transactions which system was not able to reconcile, user reconcile those transactions manually.</p><p>--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------</p><p>In this Blog we will see manual reconciliation and in next blogs we will see automatic bank reconciliations</p><p>Reference of Microsoft Blog - <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/finance/cash-bank-management/reconcile-bank-account">Reconcile a bank account - Finance | Dynamics 365 | Microsoft Learn</a></p><p>Manual Bank Reconciliation in D365 can be performed with the help of Bank statement provided by the bank.</p><p>In the manual bank reconciliation user has to manually reconcile all the transaction by marking them as reconciled and once all the entries are reconciled, they can mark the bank statement as reconciled.</p><p><i>You can't reconcile a bank statement with a bank account if any of the checks or deposit slip payments that the statement lists currently have a status of Pending cancellation. After a reviewer posts or rejects a check reversal or deposit slip payment cancellation, the status isn't Pending cancellation, and you can reconcile the bank account.</i></p><ol><li>Go to Cash and bank management > Bank Accounts > Bank accounts. <br />Select the bank account to reconcile with the bank statement and select Reconcile > Account reconciliation.<br /><br /></li><li>Enter information in the Bank statement date and Bank statement fields. <br />In the Ending balance field, enter the bank account ending balance for the period for which you are doing the reconciliation as per the Bank statement.<br /><br /></li><li>Select Transactions to open the Account reconciliation page.<br /><br /></li><li>For each transaction that is included on the bank statement, select the Cleared checkbox if the amount in Dynamics 365 Finance corresponds to the amount on the bank statement. <br /><br /></li><li>You can also enter or modify the value in the Bank transaction type field. To view the bank statement for a bank account, use the Bank statement report.</li></ol><p><br /></p><p>--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------</p><p><br /></p><p>Cancel bank statement reconciliation</p><p>The Cancel bank statement reconciliation functionality enables you to cancel bank statement reconciliation.
